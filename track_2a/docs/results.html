Checking claims against Swiss voting booklets with Apertus

Give it an official voting booklet (German, French or Italian) and a claim in any of the three languages. Apertus-v1.5-8B says whether the booklet supports, contradicts or doesn't settle the claim, and points to the pages it relied on.

How it works

Apertus doesn't read the whole booklet. A small search model first finds the 16 passages most related to the claim, across languages, and Apertus judges only those.

The PDF becomes short passages (about 900 characters), each tagged with its page and section.

A multilingual embedding model picks the 16 passages closest to the claim. A French claim finds German text directly, with no translation.

Each claim is in a different language from its booklet. All three were judged correctly.

“Le Conseil fédéral recommande à la Suisse de ratifier la Convention des Nations Unies relative aux droits des personnes handicapées.”
Apertus: the booklet is about the population initiative and its consequences; it says nothing about ratifying the UN convention. No pages cited.

Reading less is as good as reading everything, at a ninth of the cost

The same 60 test claims, judged two ways: Apertus given the whole booklet (~31k tokens), or only the 16 retrieved passages. The long input doesn't help an 8B model, and it is much slower and more expensive. Raw macro-F1 favours retrieval by +0.127 (95% CI +0.012 to +0.252), but part of that is our own setup: every “unparseable” answer was cut off at the 300-token output cap we set, and 5 of the 11 claims only retrieval got right are such cut-offs. Leaving out the 6 cut-off claims, the gap is +0.053 (95% CI −0.056 to +0.168): comparable accuracy, 9× fewer tokens.

How many passages to give it

The line shows how often the right passage is among the top k the search returns (dev split). At 8 passages it is missing a quarter of the time; at 16, about one time in ten. That, together with the F1 gain from 8 to 16 on dev (+0.041, 95% CI −0.005 to +0.091, suggestive on its own), is why we chose 16, before touching the test split. The test score (0.891) later matched dev (0.890).

Table view
  • Retrieval misses. For 70 of 652 test claims that the booklet settles (10.7 %), the annotated passage never reaches Apertus. That isn't a hard limit: 44 of those 70 were still judged correctly from other passages.
  • Multi-vote booklets. One booklet covers several votes; the search sometimes returns passages about the wrong vote (e.g. pension reform for a farming claim).
  • Prompt tweaks didn't fix contradictions. An extra rule (“if the passages cover the point and differ, it's a contradiction”) lowered dev F1 from 0.890 to 0.864, so it was dropped.
  • Data changes tested after the test run (dev only, not adopted): better passage cutting raised retrieval recall@16 from 0.911 to 0.926 but not the F1 (0.890 → 0.878, within noise); restricting the search to one vote section lowered recall to 0.823.
  • Deliberately not used: fine-tuning on six dev vote dates, XNLI/ANLI (non-commercial licence, no Italian in XNLI), and laws or debates as evidence (the question is what the booklet says).
  • Next: let Apertus pick the vote from the booklet's table of contents first, and re-rank passages before it sees them.
