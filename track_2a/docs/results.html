Checking claims against Swiss voting booklets with Apertus

Give it an official voting booklet (German, French or Italian) and a claim in any of the three languages. Apertus-v1.5-8B says whether the booklet supports, contradicts or doesn't settle the claim, and points to the pages it relied on.

Why: before every federal vote, each Swiss household gets the Federal Council's official explanations. This lets anyone check a statement from a debate, an ad or an article against what that booklet actually says, and open the page to verify it.

What the numbers on this page mean
Macro-F1
Accuracy that weighs the three answers (supports, contradicts, doesn't settle) equally, so a system can't score well by favouring one. Here it is almost the same as the share of claims answered correctly.
95% interval (CI)
The range the score would plausibly fall in with a different sample of claims of the same kind. Overlapping intervals mean a difference may be chance.
Dev and test
Dev (6 votes) was used for every design choice. Test (14 other votes) was run once at the end, so its score is not tuned to it.
Prompt tokens
How much text Apertus has to read per claim; this drives cost and time.

How it works

Apertus doesn't read the whole booklet. A small search model first finds the 16 passages most related to the claim, across languages, and Apertus judges only those.

The PDF becomes short passages (about 900 characters), each tagged with its page and section.

A multilingual embedding model picks the 16 passages closest to the claim. A French claim finds German text directly, with no translation.

Reading less is as good as reading everything, at a ninth of the cost

The same 60 test claims, judged two ways: Apertus given the whole booklet (~31k tokens), or only the 16 retrieved passages. With the whole booklet, 6 of 60 answers never finish: Apertus starts listing passage numbers 150, 151, 152, … and never writes its label. Doubling the output budget didn't help (6 failures again). Counting those as “doesn't settle”, retrieval leads by +0.127 (95% CI +0.012 to +0.252); leaving them out, by +0.053 (95% CI −0.056 to +0.168). Comparable accuracy at worst, more reliable, 9× fewer tokens.

How many passages to give it

How often the right passage is among the top k the search returns (dev split). At 8 passages it is missing a quarter of the time; at 16, about one time in ten. That, together with the F1 gain from 8 to 16 on dev, is why we chose 16, before touching the test split. A better passage cutter (orange) finds the passage more often, but did not raise F1.

How sure can we be?

Every dev score with its 95% interval. Running the chosen setup twice, unchanged, gave 0.890 and 0.864 (shaded): GPU inference is not bit-exact even at temperature 0. Two of the three variants land inside that gap and k=8 just below it, so none of them is clearly distinguishable from the chosen setup on F1 alone. The test score is a single run on 976 claims, hence its narrower interval.

  • Multi-vote booklets. One booklet covers several votes; the search sometimes returns passages about the wrong vote (e.g. pension reform for a farming claim).
  • Prompt tweaks didn't fix contradictions. An extra rule (“if the passages cover the point and differ, it's a contradiction”) lowered dev F1 from 0.890 to 0.864, so it was dropped.
  • Data changes tested after the test run (dev only, not adopted): better passage cutting raised retrieval recall@16 from 0.911 to 0.926 but not the F1 (0.890 → 0.878, within noise); restricting the search to one vote section lowered recall to 0.823.
  • Reruns aren't identical. Running the chosen setup again on dev changed 5 of 150 answers (F1 0.890 → 0.864), even at temperature 0: GPU batching isn't bit-exact. Differences of ~0.03 between single runs are noise.
  • Deliberately not used: fine-tuning on six dev vote dates, XNLI/ANLI (non-commercial licence, no Italian in XNLI), and laws or debates as evidence (the question is what the booklet says).
  • Next: stop runaway evidence lists (label first, short list), let Apertus pick the vote from the table of contents, re-rank passages.
